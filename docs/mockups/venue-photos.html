The new photographs on /barcelona/ and /sant-adria-de-besos/

Three layouts for one new section, placed after "Dónde entrenamos". Each is shown on both pages with the real section heading and styles. No photograph is edited: every crop is CSS on the whole file, a focal point plus a zoom, so the empty part of a large room stays outside the frame and the file can be re-cropped later in one line of data.

The colour grade at the top applies to everything. Espronceda is a black room under cold light and Marina-Besòs is pink tatami and orange brick, so one filter has to pull a cold and a warm set towards each other. B is my recommendation: it takes the pink down to a dusty rose and gives the black room some warmth, and the two pages then read as one association. A is the grade the site already uses on its venue cards, and is too weak for the pink. C is the strongest and the most "designed".

1 · Mosaic

An editorial mosaic, five photographs, no interaction. One tall photograph of people carries the block; the room and three details sit beside it. Every tile is a crop chosen for it, so the two people fill their frame and the empty floor stays outside. On a phone it becomes two columns and drops the fifth tile.

  • Fastest to read, no JavaScript, nothing to operate.
  • Shows five of the photographs; the rest wait for a lightbox (GLightbox is already self-hosted for the seminars page) or stay unpublished.

2 · Strip

A strip that runs off the right edge of the page. Portraits and landscapes keep their own shapes at one height, the strip snaps to each photograph, and the arrows and the count make it operable with a keyboard. It opens on the room, then the practice.

  • Shows seven or eight photographs in the height of one.
  • The cost: what is off-screen is easy to never see, and it is the one proposal with a control to build and test (keyboard, screen reader, reduced motion).

3 · The room, then the practice Recommended

The room as the room, the people as details. The empty room is not a weakness to hide, it is the answer to "what is this place like", so it gets the large frame and a label that says so. The training photographs are cropped square and tight, on hands, faces and the top of a throw, where two people look like a practice and not like an empty hall.

  • Solves the "two people in a large room" problem by design rather than by luck of cropping.
  • Survives the day there are photographs of a full class: the quad simply gets better pictures.
  • Five photographs, no JavaScript.

Search and images, whichever is chosen

  • Real <img> elements, never CSS backgrounds: a background image is invisible to Google Images.
  • Descriptive file names under the site convention, e.g. access-information-NdxqmVbV-cxem-espronceda-practice-01.jpg and .webp, generated at 480/800/base by tools/image-widths.py.
  • Alt text in four languages that says what is in the photograph and where ("Dos aikidokas practican una proyección en la sala de tatami del CxEM Espronceda, Barcelona"), plus the short visible caption.
  • width and height on every image (from _data/imgdim.yml) so nothing shifts, loading="lazy" on all of them since they sit below the fold.
  • sizes multiplied by the zoom. A tile drawn at 300px with a 1.5x crop needs a 450px file; a sizes that ignores the zoom fetches one size too small and the crop goes soft.
  • The photographs join the sitemap's image entries for the page they appear on, and the venue's schema.org Place gets a photo list of ImageObjects with creditText, creator and copyrightNotice, which is what Google Images uses for its "Licensable" details.
  • One list in data, not in templates: the photographs, their focal points and their alt texts live once in _data/venues.yml under each venue, so the town page and the new venue page read the same list and cannot drift.
