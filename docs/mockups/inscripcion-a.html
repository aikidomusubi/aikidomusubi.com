A · Plan first (not chosen: B was, on 14 October 2026)

The order of /cuotas/, continued: the fee page's buttons preselect the period and the age group, so the person lands with the first question already answered and carries on down one page. A receipt beside the form totals every line as it changes and shows exactly what is charged and when.

Household shown: two people aged 17 or over and one up to 16, monthly: 39 + 24 + 14 = 77 € a month, the spec's fourth worked example. Signing up on 20 January 2027, so today's charge is the 16 days to the 5th and the first full fee is on 5 February.

Without JavaScript the page is the same form with no live total: the period and the people are submitted, the server prices them, and Stripe's last screen states the amount. The six person blocks are <details> elements, so "add another person" works with no script at all. The spec allows this; the mockup shows the enhanced state.

What it gets right

  • specContinuity with /cuotas/. The person chose a plan there; here it is already chosen.
  • specEverything visible at once. Nothing hides behind a "next", which is the easiest shape for keyboard, screen reader and 200 % zoom.
  • moreThe timeline in the receipt. With proration the first month is two charges: 38,70 € today, then 77 € on 5 February. A single "77 €" would be the first surprise on a bank statement. The spec asks for "the date and amount of the first charge"; this shows both charges.
  • moreStruck-through full price beside each discounted line, so the family discount is seen being applied, not just stated.

Where it is weak

  • The period is chosen before the household is known. The cards can only show the price for one person aged 17 or over. A family of three compares 39 € against 99 € and has to work out the rest itself, or watch the receipt.
  • The rate is per household. The spec's form asks for it once, which cannot express a household where one person holds a CxEM membership and the others pay the general fee. Its own pricing rule (section 3.2) says that household exists.
  • The receipt is a second column. On a phone it moves to the end, so the total is out of sight while the form is filled in.

Phone, after a failed submit

The error summary takes focus and links to each field; every error sits next to its own field. The same messages come back from the server when JavaScript is off (spec 5.5).

On a phone

One column. The receipt comes after the people and before the button, so it is read at the moment of deciding. It is not sticky: a sticky total on a 667px screen covers the field being typed into, which WCAG 2.2 forbids (2.4.11), and the spec says so.

All targets are at least 24px; the chips and checkboxes are 32 to 40px.
