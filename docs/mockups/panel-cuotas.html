Panel de cuotas · the dojo's back office

For the person at the dojo who looks after memberships, who is not the person who built them. Three questions, three screens: what do I have to do, who are our members, what is going on with this family. Spanish, plain words, one obvious button per thing to do.

Nothing here can break anything. The everyday actions (form received, keikogi handed over, card checked) are one tap with Deshacer for a few seconds after. The three that change what a family pays or whether they stay (card not valid, moving to 17+, cancelling) open a confirmation that says exactly what will happen, with the amounts. Money itself (payments, refunds, the card or account) stays in Stripe, one button away.

The first screen

A greeting and the number of things to do, then four figures for orientation, then the to-do list grouped by kind, each group with its colour, an icon, one line saying what it means, and a count.

Each task says who and what

The person, the family, how long it has been waiting, and one button that says what it does: Recibida, Entregado, Comprobado. Nothing to type.

New: the paper forms

Today nothing records them. Each signup now creates a "form to collect" per person, and the panel and the monthly digest list the ones still missing.

Payment problems are information, not tasks

Stripe retries on its own. The panel shows the family and the amount and links to Stripe; it does not ask the volunteer to do anything with money.

Search first

By name, surname or email: at the dojo the question is always "is this person paid up?". Filters below for the lists people ask for: anything pending, payments pending, cancellations, sports-centre rate.

One card per membership

The plan in words ("Trimestral · 183 € · 3 personas"), the next charge, who trains, and coloured chips: green for done, orange for something to do, red for a payment problem, blue for information.

The family, on the right

Each person with their own status; the plan; a short history in plain words; and the actions, safest first. Contact details are here because this is a logged-in tool for the dojo; they never go into emails.

What it does not do

Adding people or changing the period stays a request to info@, because the family price has to be recalculated. It says so, rather than hiding the option.

Pressing Entregado turns the row green, and a bar at the bottom offers Deshacer for a few seconds. The tab bar keeps the three screens under the thumb.

No password to remember

Cloudflare Access guards the panel: the volunteer types their email and gets a 6-digit code. Only the addresses you list can get in; adding or removing a volunteer is one line in Cloudflare. We store no password.

An empty list is good news

"Todo al día", and one line on when the panel checks for new things, so an empty screen never looks broken.

Ayuda

The third tab: short answers to "a family wants to leave", "someone says they paid twice", "how do I add someone", in the panel's own words, each ending with who to ask.
