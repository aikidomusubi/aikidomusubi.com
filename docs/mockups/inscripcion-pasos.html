Four steps on our site, then Stripe's page, then back. Desktop at 1000px on the left and a phone at 375px on the right; the phone often shows a different state of the same step, an error or the other path, so one mockup covers two cases.

The household throughout: Ana and Marc, 17 or over, and Lía, up to 16, quarterly: 99 + 54 + 30 = 183 € a quarter. They sign up on Wednesday 20 January 2027, pay 30,99 € today for the 16 days to the 5th, and 183 € every quarter from 5 February. Their 14 days to withdraw end on 3 February.

What it asks

Name, surnames and the age group, per person. Month and year of birth only for "up to 16", because that is what the age change at 17 needs and nothing more.

The rate is chosen per person: general for everybody unless someone holds a sports-centre membership. Ana and Marc are on the general fee.

On the phone: the other path

Lía is drawn with the sports-centre rate picked, to show what it asks: which centre, then its conditions right there.

The choices follow the facts: Marina-Besòs is offered only to "up to 16", because its only class is for up to 16, and CxEM Espronceda only from 12. The validation on the server says the same.

Accessibility

Each person is a fieldset with its own legend. "Add another person" moves focus to the new person's first field; "remove" names who it removes.

Without JavaScript, persons 2 to 6 are closed <details> blocks, "Add person 2" and so on, and the four steps are one page.

The name, asked once

The payer is picked from the people already listed, so our page never asks a name twice. Only "Otra persona", somebody who pays and does not train, gets name fields, because that name has not been typed yet.

The declarations stay in the first person and name who is making them, because the person filling this in is the payer: "I am an adult", "I am Lía's parent or guardian".

What Stripe will still ask

Stripe's own page asks for the name on the card, or the account holder for SEPA. We cannot fill it in. Stripe only fills it from a card the customer has saved before, and loading Stripe's script on our page to do it would break the no-third-party rule. It is also a different fact: the account may be in somebody else's name. We stop Stripe asking for the customer's name separately, because we already send it.

On the phone

A grandparent paying for the family: "Otra persona", then their own name, email and phone, and the same two declarations.

Catalan pages add one line here: Stripe has no Catalan, so its page and its receipts are in Spanish.

The last screen that is ours

Every line, every date in order, what the fee does not cover, the renewal and cancellation promises, then the two checkboxes, the first layer of the privacy notice and the button. This is what consumer law wants in front of somebody before they pay (spec 11.1).

The last day to withdraw is in the timeline, as a date, not as "14 days".

Coming back from Stripe

The blue notice is the cancel return: somebody pressed "back" on Stripe's page. Nothing was charged; the answers come back from sessionStorage, never from the URL, and are cleared after a successful payment.

On the phone: an email that already pays

Without this check a parent adding a second child would open a second subscription, both at full price, and the family discount would be lost. The server checks the email against active subscriptions and stops there.

It does tell anyone who types that address that it has a membership. That is a small disclosure, and I think it is the right trade. confirm

Not ours, but configured by us

Stripe draws this page. We set the logo and colours, the language (es, en, ja; Catalan falls back to Spanish), the lines, the date of the first full charge, and the yellow message, which is the spec's custom_text. It states that confirming creates a recurring payment and gives the dates.

What the payer types here

The card, or the IBAN and the account holder's name for SEPA, and the country. The email arrives already filled in and cannot be edited. The customer's name is not asked, because we have sent it.

The checkbox

Stripe's own terms-of-service checkbox, required by the spec, points at our membership terms page. Combined with ours on step 4, the acceptance is recorded twice, which is what evidence wants.

One page for every case

The return URL carries no session ID and no personal data (spec 6.5), so the page cannot know who arrived or what they chose. It says what happens next in a form that is true for everybody: SEPA "if", keikogi "if". The personal details are in the confirmation email.

The paper still matters

The registration form is the only place the ID number, the date of birth, health information and the image consent live, by design. So it is step 2 here, and it links to the forms already on /recursos/.

That form may need a change: its description on /recursos/ says it covers the fee. Anything about choosing the fee or giving bank details goes, now that both happen online. See tab 2.

Not indexed

The thank-you page carries sitemap: false, so it is out of the sitemap and noindex. Analytics is not loaded on it or on the signup page (spec 10.3); the four steps stay indexable.
